Transforming the whole picture:  

Erase / Paint: drag over the image — click then Shift-click joins the two points with a straight line (Photoshop style), and Shift-drag locks the stroke to horizontal / vertical. Fill: click inside a shape to flood it with colour. Clone: click to set the source, then drag to stamp that area elsewhere (Alt+click re-sets the source) — once the source is set, the ring under your pointer shows the exact patch that will be stamped, so you can line it up before you press. History: brush the original picture back by hand. Select / Lasso / Wand: mark out an area, then fill it, move it, delete it, or use AI content-aware fill (Esc deselects). Delete (the key, or 🧽) cuts the selection away to transparent — so with the 🪄 wand you can Alt+click a colour to take it across the whole picture and delete every pixel of it in one go, which is how you get a clean transparent background on a card art or a sprite. The ⬜/▨ Erase to button says whether Erase and the paint bucket paint white or cut through; it starts on transparent when you open the editor from a Card Art slot and on white for a question's picture. Move: drag inside a selection to cut it out and drop it anywhere (Alt to copy), or drag a text label to reposition it. Resize: drag the handles round the box to make the selected object bigger or smaller — corners resize both ways, edges one way, and the corner opposite the one you drag stays exactly where it is. Drag inside the box to move it, hold Shift to swap between keeping the shape and stretching it freely, or type a width / height percentage in the panel. Rotate / Skew: turn or slant the selected object — or, with nothing selected, the whole picture, which is how you straighten a page that went into the scanner crooked. Drag on the image (Shift snaps to 15°) or use the sliders, and 📐 Straighten lets you trace a line along a crooked edge to level it in one go. Enter applies the turn, Esc cancels it. Line: drag from start to end. Text: click, type, then Enter.
📋 Paste a picture: press Ctrl+V (⌘V) — or the 📋 button — to drop whatever picture is on the clipboard straight onto this one. It lands scaled to fit inside the picture, with the resize handles already on it: drag a corner to size it, drag the middle to move it, turn or slant it if you want, then ✓ Apply to burn it in (Esc takes it away again).
Scroll over the image to zoom (down to single pixels); hold Space (or the middle mouse button) and drag to pan; scroll over either slider to change it. Set the brush to 1 px for pixel-perfect edits.
Brush size: Erase, Paint, Clone, History and Line draw a ring around your pointer at the exact size of the mark they will make, at whatever zoom you are on — so you can see the brush before you use it. Change the size with the slider, the wheel over it, or [ and ], and the size is shown on the picture as you change it. With Clone the ring is filled with the pixels it is about to stamp — move the pointer and the preview moves with it, so a patch is aimed by looking at it rather than by counting across from the source pin.
✨ Regenerate: type what you want in the purple bar and the AI redraws it. Select an area first and only that area can change; with nothing selected the whole picture is redrawn. It is one step either way, so ↶ Undo puts the picture straight back if the AI missed.
